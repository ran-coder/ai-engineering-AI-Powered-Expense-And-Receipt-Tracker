

| Project Name | AI-Powered Expense and Receipt Tracker |

| Domain  | Finance |
| Problem Statement  | Individuals or small businesses rely on traditional ways of tracking their expenses such as logging in the notes or sheets on a daily basis based on receipts and memory. This approach is slow and prone to human mistakes, making it critical for small businesses needing visibility of where their money goes. Without a proper and accurate tracking, people suffer with overspending and friction around financial planning.  Moreover, many users spread their money across several banks and e-wallets and pay using cash, cards, and QR codes. Each banking or e-wallet app records only the transactions made through its own platform. As a result, users lack a consolidated, categorized view of their spending across payment methods. |
| Evidence of the Problem  | Manual expense tracking through spreadsheets or paper creates bottlenecks, especially as companies scale (SoftCo, 2025). Reasons why it’s important to address: (1) Spreadsheets and emails are error-prone and time-consuming. (2) Manual matching and validation delay month-end close.(3) Inconsistent data leads to inaccurate forecasting.  [https\://softco.com/blog/7-expense-management-problems-costing-you-time-money/](https://softco.com/blog/7-expense-management-problems-costing-you-time-money/)  According to The Edge Malaysia (2022), managing expenses has become increasingly complex and inconvenient for the author due to the fragmentation of funds across multiple e-wallets (such as Touch ’n Go, Grab, and 1Pay), physical cards, bank accounts, and emergency cash. Unlike relying on traditional cash, tracking daily spending now requires constant monitoring of various digital balances and physical card top-ups, a burden the author expects will only grow as more retailers launch their own proprietary payment apps. | Engineering Gap / Limitation  | Existing receipt scanners and bank apps each cover only part of a person’s spending, and the reviewed approaches do not combine receipt items, QR payment confirmations, and bank records into one categorized view with grounded, personalized insights. |
| Initial Project Objectives (2–3)  | O1: To investigate the current limitations, performance metrics, and technological requirements of existing expense trackers.  O2: To develop an AI-powered expense tracker, using Malaysian receipt and QR payment data, that consolidates records from multiple bank and e-wallet apps and provides grounded, personalized insights through retrieval-based question answering. O3: To evaluate the solution in terms of answer quality, groundedness, latency, and safety.   |
| Target Users  | Small business owners and digitally active consumers who frequently use mobile banking and e-wallet solutions.  |
| Data Sources (at least 2\)  | **ICDAR2019 SROIE**: contains 987 receipt images in English, annotated with key fields (company, date, address, and total) and bounding boxes with transcripts for all text regions in the images. [https\://huggingface.co/datasets/jsdnrs/ICDAR2019-SROIE](https://huggingface.co/datasets/jsdnrs/ICDAR2019-SROIE)  |
| Similar to EduAdvisor  | Both use retrieval-augmented generation, so answers are grounded in stored documents or records and not in the LLM’s memory, which reduces hallucination. Both personalize their output to the individual (a student’s situation, a user’s spending). |
| Different from EduAdvisor  | **Data source:** EduAdvisor retrieves from shared institutional text such as policies and calendars. This tracker retrieves from each user’s private records, which was created from receipts and payment screenshots. **Extra ML pipeline:** This system needs OCR, information extraction, and categorization models that you train and evaluate while EduAdvisor only uses LLM. |
| Responsible AI Risks (at least 2\)  | **Privacy of financial data** \- Receipts and screenshots reveal spending habits, merchants, names, and account details  **Inaccurate extraction or insights** \- An OCR error produces wrong totals and misleading insights |
